# qabench ledger — every defect in the kit, and what it taught

**EVERY RECORDED CONCLUSION IN THIS TREE IS DATED, AND NOTHING RE-READS ANY OF THEM.** That is the most general statement this ledger reached, on 2026-09-20, and most of the classes below are instances of it. Four forms turned up in one day across two codebases, independently: a mutation watched red two days earlier that had silently stopped being able to fail; a reason asserting another row's status that had since changed; a line number that drifted 333 lines because the file grew above it; and a row's REVIEW itself, which goes stale when a row it depends on is reviewed later. All four were true when recorded, none was careless, and no amount of re-reading shows any of them. The instruments are the enforcement.

One row per error the kit itself produced or failed to see. The kit is the one copy six projects run, so a defect here is a defect in six places at once, and the generalisation is the deliverable — the fix is the smaller half.

A FALSE POSITIVE IN THE AUDIT AND A GAP IN THE SUITE ARE DIFFERENT FAILURES and are not recorded in the same place: the first is a calibration row against the audit, the second a ledger row against the suite. ana nearly shipped a fix for a defect that did not exist (a descriptor declared a field an editable select2; the server serves it disabled and nobody can edit it), found when a browser guard's `fill` timed out on a disabled input — its defect count went DOWN by one. Merging the two makes an audit that is finding a lot indistinguishable from one that is inventing a lot.

A SCALED COUNT IS AN UPPER BOUND until one instance has been read by hand, and is labelled as such wherever it is reported. On 2026-09-20 nine confident scaled heuristics were beaten by targeted reads, twice agreeing with the wrong answer; a sweep for marker-keyed guards reported 35, three were read by hand, and all three were false positives. Every number that held up that day came from a structural derivation or from reading the sites.

A finding carries THREE NUMBERS where they differ, never one: what was DEMONSTRATED, how much is BROKEN, and how much of that is HARMFUL. ACT-07 is why — 12 of 146 dialogs broken, 7 of those harmful, and zero harm on the single one a browser happened to demonstrate, because that dialog persists every keystroke and restores it. Any one number alone prioritises the row wrong, in a direction you cannot predict.
